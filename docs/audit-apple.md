# DigiLocker Concept: "Apple" design audit (Phase 1, audit only)

Lens: Apple's fluid-interface principles (response, direct manipulation, interruptibility, springs, velocity handoff, spatial consistency, materials, typography, feedback, reduced motion). I walked every screen at 1440x900 and on the mobile preset (375x812), in light and dark: landing, sign in with OTP autofill, wallet, document sheet, stack sheet and drill-in, Get documents (error and success), Share (QR, link, user), Shared and revoke, Drive, the ⌘K palette, the profile menu and toasts. Where a real gesture couldn't be triggered, I judged it from the code.

**What's already good:** pointer-down press scales, origin-aware popovers, the Vaul-style sheet curve `cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1)`, toasts built on transitions, a palette with no animation, the blur-bridged label morph, hold-to-remove that is slow to commit and quick to cancel, an auto-hide on revealed data, focus return on close, tabular countdowns. The basics are sound. Most of what follows separates "polished web app" from "feels physical".

Measured facts referenced below:
- **Stack affordance:** at rest, `.wc-l1` peeks **1 px** and `.wc-l2` **3 px** below the card (measured `getBoundingClientRect`: wc bottom 792, l1 793, l2 795).
- **Type scale:** **20 distinct font sizes** (10 → 30px, half-pixel steps) and **7 weights**. 550/650/750 are declared, but the font only loads 400/500/600/700/800 static instances.
- **Radii:** **16 distinct border radii**.
- **iOS press states:** **0 `touchstart` listeners**, so iOS Safari never applies `:active`.
- **Accessibility media queries:** no `prefers-reduced-transparency` or `prefers-contrast`, and no `dvh` anywhere.

## Breakthrough ideas

These five are all feasible in vanilla JS with the spring helper from #9, and each makes one moment memorable instead of polishing everything evenly.

### 1. A physical card stack on mobile, with a shared-element open
On phones, show the wallet like Apple Wallet: cards overlap so only each card's top 64px (issuer and title) is visible, in one continuous pile per category. Tapping a card **lifts it out of the pile** (others slide down and dim) and **morphs it into the sheet's hero position** (FLIP across layers: measure the card rect, clone it into `#overlay-root`, spring `x/y/scale` to the `.doc-hero` rect with `damping .9, response .42`, then cross-fade to the real sheet). Drag the hero card down and the sheet follows; release and the card **flies back into its slot** using the release velocity. Stacks (B.Tech, CBSE) fan open in place with an 18px offset per item on tap, before you drill in. This replaces a grid where only 1.5 cards fit on screen with a pile you can see whole.

### 2. Light-reactive cards with a live hologram seal
Track `pointermove` on desktop and `deviceorientation` on phones (behind a tap-to-enable permission on iOS). Drive `--rx/--ry` (±6°) and a specular position `--lx/--ly` on `.wc`: `transform:perspective(900px) rotateX(var(--rx)) rotateY(var(--ry))` plus a `radial-gradient(circle at var(--lx) var(--ly), rgba(255,255,255,.35), transparent 40%)` with `mix-blend-mode:soft-light`. Return to rest on a spring. Turn the "Verified" badge into a small **holographic foil seal** (`conic-gradient` rainbow masked to a shield, its hue tied to tilt). This is a real trust signal: a moving seal is hard to fake with a screenshot, which suits an officer-facing ID. Turn it off under reduced motion, where the seal becomes a static gradient.

### 3. "Present" mode: flip the card to show its QR
"Show QR" from a document runs a full-screen takeover. The card scales up from its position, then **flips on Y (rotateY 180°, spring `damping .8`)** to reveal the QR on its back, on a pure-white surface. A draining **expiry ring** traces the QR's rounded border (an SVG `stroke-dashoffset` driven by the countdown), and `navigator.wakeLock.request('screen')` keeps the display on. When the share is "scanned" (simulate after ~4s in the prototype), the ring snaps closed and the back flashes a check with `navigator.vibrate?.(10)`: **"Seaside Hotel saw Name, Photo, Age 18+"**. That closes the loop the current flow leaves open. Swipe down to put the card back.

### 4. Sharing as live redaction
In the Share modal, put a **miniature of the actual document** above the switches, with each field rendered as a row. Flipping a switch off slides a black redaction bar across that row (`clip-path` inset on a spring, 260ms) and the summary's "4 of 7" **rolls to the new number** (tabular digits in a vertical translate wheel). The Aadhaar "Age over 18" row shows the birth date redacted and a green "18+ ✓" stamped over it. Turning switches into visible redaction makes "share only what's needed" (the product's core promise) something you can see.

### 5. Gesture-native sheets with an iOS-style card stack
Rebuild `enableDrag` on the spring helper as a reusable `Sheet` that: tracks 1:1 from the header **and** from the body when scrolled to the top; rubber-bands past the top; projects momentum (`current + (v/1000)·.998/(1-.998)`) to choose between dismiss, half-height and full detents; and hands velocity into a `damping .85` spring. While it's open, the page behind **scales to .94 with 14px corners and darkens**, all bound to drag progress. A second sheet (Share opened from a document) pushes the first back again, like iOS stacked sheets. Every swap animates height (#6) and direction (#7), so the navigation feels like one physical object rather than a series of re-rendered dialogs.

## Phase 3 review (Apple lens, second pass)

Reviewed the whole of `index.html` (CSS and JS read end to end) and walked every flow in a visible Chrome tab. That window was locked at 500px wide, so 375 and 1440 were run inside a same-origin iframe of exactly that size, scripted and panned at 1:1 for screenshots. Light and dark were both checked.

### What landed well
- **Living card.** The flight lands on the sheet hero within 1px on desktop and mobile. Swipe-to-dismiss flies the card home. When the slot is off-screen, the card fades back instead of flying.
- **Sheet physics.** Tracking is 1:1. Upward drag rubber-bands (200px of drag gives 96px of travel). Snap-back uses a damping .85 spring with no visible overshoot. Dismissal is velocity-projected. The app behind tracks drag progress and resets cleanly.
- **Recipient lens.** The CSS and JS agree: the value sits under each field, a redaction bar sweeps over fields that are off, Aadhaar shows an "18+" stamp, and the count rolls in the direction it moved. Create turns the list over into the QR, the expiry ring drains, and Present → simulated scan works. The previous reviewer's half-finished "merged lens" is fully wired: no orphaned classes (the only unused rule, `.tag-new`, was removed).
- **Restraint.** Tilt is ±6° with a soft-light glare, and the foil seal only moves with tilt. The palette, popovers and toasts stay calm.

### What I changed
1. **Shared-element flight (`flyPair`).** Before, the card was a single scaled clone, so the title and corner radius popped at the end. For example, the 18px mobile card title landed at about 14px and then jumped to 22px. Now the source clone and the target's own face fly along the same path, and the target fades in on top. The card lands as exactly what is there. Used for lift, land and arrival.
2. **Hero tilt was dead on desktop.** `.doc-hero .wc-btn{pointer-events:none}` made the hero impossible to hit-test, so it never tilted. It is now interactive (`cursor:default`), and a tap plays one foil sweep (`.wc.shine`).
3. **(b) Device orientation.** Android streams orientation as before. iOS asks for permission once, from a tap on the open card, which is when the person is looking at it. It is off under reduced motion. The tilt loop is now frame-rate independent (same feel at 60 and 120Hz). It no longer keeps a rAF running for settled or detached cards, which was an infinite loop once a sheet closed while orientation was active.
4. **Release velocity bug.** If the finger stopped before lifting, `end()` fell back to the whole drag's average velocity. Example: a slow 80px pull, a pause, then release dismissed the sheet. Velocity now uses the last 80ms measured up to *now*, so a finger that stopped hands over about 0.
5. **(a) Tabbar.** The tabbar is already a sibling of `.app`, so it stays put while the app recedes (checked: rect unchanged, z-index under the scrim). I also split `body.recede` from `body.locked`, so the ⌘K palette (which deliberately never animates) no longer makes the app recede on mobile.
6. **Stacked sheets peek.** On mobile, a pushed parent sheet now scales from its top edge (`translateY(-12px) scale(.94)`). It peeks above the sheet on top instead of hiding behind it.
7. **(c) Count-up.** Moved into `countUp()`. A hidden tab waits for `visibilitychange`. Hiding mid-count lands the final value, and there is a 1.2s backstop. The markup always holds the real number.
8. **(d) "Show me where".** The collapsed content is `inert` and `aria-hidden`, and the toggle has `aria-controls`.
9. **Route cross-fade froze the closing sheet.** Sign-in → wallet ran a View Transition while the sign-in sheet was still sliding away, so it ghosted in the snapshot. Routes now skip the cross-fade while any layer is still animating out, and the sheet slides down over the new page.
10. **Present mode in dark.** The panel now carries light-theme tokens, which fixes the pale "Concept · demo QR" tag and the low-contrast green checks. Its inner element was renamed `.present-body`, because the old `.present` also matched the panel and applied double padding and centring to it.
11. **Horizontal scroll at 375.** `.app` and `.wallet-layout` used `1fr`, i.e. `minmax(auto,1fr)`. Once a 4th filter chip appeared, or on the Shared tabs, the scrollers' min-content widened the page to 437–548px. Both are now `minmax(0,1fr)`.
12. **Smaller fixes.**
    - Landing nav overflowed at 375 ("Sign in" ran 15px past the gutter), fixed with tighter nav padding and an icon-only language button.
    - The arrival ring no longer flattens the card's edge light (`--wc-shadow` is kept inside the keyframes).
    - Duplicate `.qr-wrap` rules were merged.
    - Redacted lens values are `aria-hidden`.
    - A removed card no longer flies home into a gap that is closing; its slot fades out where it is.

### Verdict
The motion now has one consistent physical model. Springs handle anything you touch, the drawer curve handles everything else, every flight starts from what's on screen and lands on what's there, and the layered depth (app → parent sheet → modal) reads like iOS. Nothing feels bouncy for its own sake. I'd ship it as a concept. I skipped wallet deck mode on purpose: it would add a second wallet layout to maintain, and the grid plus living card already carries the moment.
